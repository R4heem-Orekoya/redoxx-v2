## What Being On-Call Taught Me

Six months of primary on-call. Zero sleep, lots of pagers, and a few hard-won lessons.

## Downtime is Inevitable

No matter how good your tests are, production will find a way to break. The question isn't _if_ something will fail, but _how fast_ you can recover.

## The Runbook Mindset

Every incident I resolved, I wrote down exactly what I did. Over time, those notes became runbooks. And those runbooks became the difference between a 10-minute outage and a 60-minute one.

```yaml
runbook:
  service: auth-api
  symptoms:
    - "5xx errors > 1%"
    - "latency p99 > 2s"
  steps:
    - check: "Dashboard: auth-api-error-rate"
    - action: "Rollback last deploy"
      command: "kubectl rollout undo deployment/auth-api"
    - verify: "Error rate drops below threshold"
    - escalate: "If still failing, ping #infra"
```

## What I Learned

- **Alert fatigue is real** — Only page for things the engineer can actually fix
- **Post-mortems should be blameless** — Root cause is almost always systemic
- **Sleep is not optional** — A tired engineer makes worse decisions than a broken system

> The goal of on-call isn't to prevent every incident. It's to make every incident boring.

Boring incidents mean you have good runbooks, good monitoring, and a team that knows what to do.
